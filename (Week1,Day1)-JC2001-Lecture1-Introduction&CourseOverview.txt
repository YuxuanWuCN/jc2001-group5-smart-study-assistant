Software Product – Software Product or Software as a Product (SaaP) is made 
to be sold to users.
Prototype software – Independent ‘in-house programs’ developed by few (one 
or two) people may implement some exciting features, but these are usually not 
sold to users. These kinds of software are known as prototype software (or 
sometimes also referred to as 'homemade software'/ 'amateur software'), but 
not products.

What is Software Engineering?


=== Page 21 ===
Software Engineering is a collection of concepts, methods, and tools which 
focuses on the techniques to build large and complex software.
•
Large means that it requires many people working together. We need to 
learn how to organise people to work together.
•
Complex means that the software has multiple components which affect 
each other. We need to learn how to architect software and reduce its 
complexity.

Case: Therac-25
•
Therac-25 was a computer-controlled radiation therapy 
machine produced by Atomic Energy of Canada 
Limited (AECL) in 1982.
•
Because of concurrent programming errors (also known 
as race conditions), it sometimes gave its patients 
radiation doses that were hundreds of times greater than 
normal.
•
Patients were therefore death or serious injured.

In 1995, the Standish Group research institute took 8,000 software projects in 
the United States as a survey sample. The survey results showed that 84% of 
software plans could not be completed within the set time and budget, and 
more than 30% of the projects were cancelled during implementation. 
The average project budget over 189%.

=== Page 27 ===
Typically, software products have 6 
development processes:
Planning – decide what software and features to develop
Requirements Capture – collect requirements from 
experts & users.
Design & Analysis – design the software architecture and 
the overall structure of the program.
Implementation – coding
Testing – test the modules of the software and test the 
software as a whole to find bugs.
Maintenance – fix bugs reported by users

What the user really needed.
This is the feature that users really need. Usually, users 
only need very simple functions, but there is a 
communication problem. Users always want to add too 
much functionality to custom software. This is not 
because they NEED these functions, but because they 
are not familiar with their own business, and are afraid 
of missing out on functions which might be necessary.
Therefore, in the requirements analysis phase, 
software engineers need to identify as much as 
possible about the core functions of the users’ 
business.

Plagiarism
“Plagiarism” is defined by the University as the use, without adequate acknowledgment, of the 
intellectual work of another person in work submitted for assessment. A student cannot be 
found to have committed plagiarism where it can be shown that the student has taken all 
reasonable care to avoid representing the work of others as his or her own.

=== Page 58 ===
Plagiarism in reports
•
If taking text verbatim, put it in quotes and cite the source.
•
Alternatively, paraphrase and cite the source.
•
Or summarise and cite the source.
•
As a rule of thumb, don’t do this for more than 3-4 sentences.
•
See https://integrity.mit.edu/handbook/writing-original-work for an excellent guide.
