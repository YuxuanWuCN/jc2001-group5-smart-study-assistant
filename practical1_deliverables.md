#### Q1: What are three things that would instantly cheer you up?
1. Executing a multi-branch `git merge` on a complex feature release that merges cleanly with zero conflicts and passes all automated CI regression pipelines on the first push.
2. A velvety, piping-hot flat white brewed with single-origin Ethiopian beans at 08:00 AM before a productive day of systems engineering.
3. A sunset bicycle ride along the coastal promenade of Shenzhen Bay, watching the illuminated skyline reflect across the calm open waters.

#### Q2: What is your favourite dish of food, and what memories do you associate with it?
**Steamed Seafood Feast and Hand-Crafted Shrimp Dumplings (*Ha Jiao* / 虾饺)**. My fondest memory is gathering with my extended family at a heritage Cantonese teahouse in Shekou every Lunar New Year. We sat around a massive circular wooden table enveloped in fragrant jasmine tea steam and the rhythmic clatter of bamboo dim sum steamers. The delicate, translucent dumpling skins wrapping succulent tiger prawns represent not just culinary mastery, but an enduring emotional anchor of familial solidarity and shared heritage that grounds me during challenging academic semesters.

#### Q3: If you were to give a tour of your hometown, what would be the highlights?
A curated tour of **Shenzhen** would showcase the breathtaking metamorphosis of a former fishing village into a global technological megalopolis within forty years:
- **Morning**: Strolling through the Nanshan High-Tech Industrial Park, visiting open hardware robotics incubators and drone logistics corridors that power modern digital commerce.
- **Afternoon**: Finding peace beneath the century-old banyan trees of Lychee Park, followed by climbing the peak of Lianhuashan Park to view the Civic Centre and the Deng Xiaoping bronze statue.
- **Evening**: Exploring OCT-LOFT (Overseas Chinese Town Creative Cultural Park), where repurposed industrial warehouses now host contemporary art galleries, indie design studios, and open-air acoustic jazz lounges.

#### Q4: If you could witness one key moment of history, what would you choose?
**July 20, 1969 — The Apollo 11 Lunar Module Touchdown on the Sea of Tranquility**. I would stand inside NASA Mission Control in Houston at the exact moment Margaret Hamilton’s asynchronous flight software recovered from executive radar overload, allowing Charlie Duke to confirm: *"Tranquility Base here. The Eagle has landed."* To witness human courage, precision mathematics, and real-time software engineering triumph under existential pressure remains my ultimate inspiration for studying software engineering.

#### Q5: Where do you go and what do you do when you need to restore your energy?
When cognitive fatigue sets in, I take my road bicycle on a solitary, two-hour night ride along the Shenzhen Bay Coastal Greenway. With a synthwave playlist flowing through my headphones and the cool sea breeze cutting through the subtropical humidity, my mind clears. I finish the ride by sitting on the granite steps overlooking the Hong Kong skyline, spending twenty minutes in quiet reflective journaling to reset my mental cache.

### 4.1 WHAT the System Does (Core Solution & Functional Scope)

The proposed system—titled **"Smart Study Assistant System for University Students"**—is a novel educational intelligence platform that bridges Knowledge Graphs (Neo4j) and Retrieval-Augmented Generation (GraphRAG) to eradicate rote memorization and diagnose student conceptual blind spots.

#### Core Functional Modules
1. **Automated Exam Question Diagnostic Ingestion (`auto_builder.py`)**:
   Unlike generic flashcard apps that store raw text, our ingestion engine utilizes Alibaba Cloud DashScope (`qwen-turbo`) acting as a hyper-critical Academic Proposition & Logic Diagnostic Expert. The LLM decomposes complex multi-choice and short-answer questions into structured JSON primitives:
   - Extracting core tested academic concepts (`核心考点`).
   - Isolating strict prerequisite boundary conditions (`定理条件`).
   - Pinpointing frequent distractors and student fallacies (`易错漏洞`).
   - Establishing directional causal relationships: `(:Concept)-[:DEPENDS_ON {type: "易错于"}]->(:Concept)`.
2. **Neo4j Diagnostic Knowledge Graph (Cloud AuraDB)**:
   The knowledge graph maintains an evolving disciplinary ontology. When multiple exam questions evaluate the same concept from differing perspectives, our Cypher `MERGE` logic accumulates distinct traps into the same conceptual node (`c.detail + ' | 补充陷阱: ' + $detail`), creating a rich, interconnected map of academic pitfalls.
3. **Academic Arbitration Gate & Knowledge Hallucination Suppression (`systematic_eval_engine.py`)**:
   A pervasive failure mode of generative AI in higher education is severe knowledge hallucination—where unguided LLMs produce nonsensical, ungrounded answers by fabricating invalid theorem premises, erroneous mathematical derivations, or non-existent counterexamples. By strictly anchoring all diagnostic dialogues to verified Neo4j conceptual subgraphs and enforcing an Academic Confidence Arbitration Gate ($\ge 85\%$), our system purges noisy and spurious causal links, directly and drastically reducing the knowledge hallucination rate in academic tutoring.
4. **Socratic Diagnostic Interrogation Tutor (`real_graph_engine.py`)**:
   Traditional AI tutors feed complete answers directly, encouraging passive consumption. In contrast, our Socratic engine is explicitly prohibited from revealing answers. Constrained by verified Neo4j topology facts, it poses targeted, sequential questions that force the student to inspect their own reasoning gaps and identify missed prerequisite conditions.
5. **Dynamic Diagnostic Quiz Engine & Mastery Visualization (`scraped_app.js` & ECharts 5.5.1)**:
   The quiz engine dynamically constructs targeted assessment items where each incorrect option directly triggers a diagnostic traversal to an underlying misconception node. Student performance is visualized in real-time through an interactive ECharts 5.5.1 five-dimensional competency radar chart (Basic Concepts, Boundary Deductions, Trap Defense, Cross-Chapter Synthesis, Computational Precision).

### 4.2 WHO the System Targets (Stakeholders & Beneficiaries)

The Smart Study Assistant System is engineered for two distinct stakeholder cohorts within the higher education ecosystem:

#### Primary Target: Undergraduate University Students
- **Context & Pain Points**: Students enrolled in intensive STEM, business, and informatics disciplines (such as BSc BMIS, Computer Science, and Economics) face heavy cognitive overload across 4–6 concurrent modules. During examination periods, students struggle with fragmented lecture slides, isolated textbook PDFs, and brute-force past paper drilling. Crucially, when students consult general AI chatbots, existing models suffer from severe academic hallucinations—giving confident but nonsensical answers that mislead students and reinforce conceptual blind spots. Furthermore, conventional past papers fail to explain *why* a specific distractor was chosen or which underlying misconception was triggered.
- **Empirical Value Delivered**:
   - Drastically curtails academic knowledge hallucination rates via deterministic Neo4j graph grounding.
   - Eliminates the frustration of rote memorization by rendering underlying concept dependencies visible.
   - Reduces examination revision time by an estimated 30–40% through targeted remediation of verified weak nodes rather than indiscriminate re-reading.
  - Builds resilient, metacognitive problem-solving habits via Socratic cross-examination.

#### Secondary Target: Course Instructors, Teaching Assistants (TAs) & Curriculum Designers
- **Context & Pain Points**: University instructors teaching large cohorts (100–300+ students) lack granular visibility into cohort-wide misconceptions until final examination scripts are graded, at which point pedagogical remediation is impossible. TAs spend dozens of hours answering identical, repetitive questions during office hours.
- **Empirical Value Delivered**:
  - Generates aggregated, anonymized cohort diagnostic heatmaps revealing which specific concepts and traps exhibit the highest failure rates across the student body.
  - Empowers instructors to adjust lecture pacing and target tutorial discussions toward proven conceptual stumbling blocks ahead of exams.
  - Provides TAs with an automated, authoritative diagnostic assistant for student query triage.

### 4.3 Major Requirements & Constraints

The engineering design and delivery of the Smart Study Assistant System are strictly bounded by three major constraint categories:

#### 1. Technical Constraints
- **Sub-2-Second Graph Retrieval Latency**: Cypher queries traversing up to two hops in Neo4j Cloud AuraDB must execute with round-trip latency under 2.0 seconds (current PoC benchmarks achieve $<800$ ms on $LIMIT\ 40$ queries) to maintain seamless conversational responsiveness.
- **LLM API Rate Limits & Token Economics**: The Alibaba Cloud DashScope (`qwen-turbo`) API imposes strict Requests-Per-Minute (RPM) limits and occasional HTTP 429 rate-limit responses. The pipeline must implement exponential backoff with jitter (`auto_builder.py:59-61`) and multi-worker thread pools (`ThreadPoolExecutor`) to guarantee resilient ingestion.
- **Strict Knowledge Noise Gate**: As demonstrated in our 12-discipline benchmark, raw LLM context injection without confidence gating results in accuracy degradation in nuanced subjects (e.g., Physics $-1.32\%$). The system must enforce the $\ge 85\%$ confidence threshold to prevent cognitive distraction.
- **Cloud Infrastructure Limits**: Development utilizes educational/free-tier cloud resources (Neo4j AuraDB Free tier limit of 200,000 nodes and 400,000 relationships; memory limit of 512 MB). The graph schema must be highly normalized and indexed.

#### 2. Time & Milestone Schedule Constraints
